# Segment Scoring

**Research date:** 23 October 2026
**Status:** Provisional analyst judgement; no customer validation

## Method

Weighted score = sum(weight x score) / 100.

Weights:
- Cross-border need: 30%.
- Capability fit: 25%.
- Buyer access: 20%.
- Pilot simplicity: 15%.
- Evidence strength: 10%.

Higher scores are more favourable. A score of 1 indicates weak fit,
difficult access or substantial uncertainty; 3 indicates mixed fit;
5 indicates strong fit or well-supported evidence.

All current scores are assumptions informed by the desk research.
They are not conversion probabilities, market measurements or proof
that one segment is commercially superior.

## Initial Scores

| Candidate | Cross-border need | Capability fit | Buyer access | Pilot simplicity | Evidence strength | Weighted score / 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Professional-services agencies | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 2 | 3.80 |
| International e-commerce firms | 4 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 3.05 |
| Software and technology-service firms | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 3.45 |

## Rationale and Uncertainty

- Cross-border need: all candidates score 4 because recurring
  international activity is part of our segment definition. This does
  not measure its prevalence or intensity in the wider market.
- Capability fit: agencies and technology services score 4 provisionally.
  E-commerce scores 3 because acquiring, marketplace settlement and
  goods restrictions may introduce requirements outside our comparison.
- Buyer access: agencies score 4 based on a hypothesis that founders
  and agency networks offer a practical route to discovery. Others
  score 3. No channel response data supports this difference yet.
- Pilot simplicity: agencies score 4 for a narrowly scoped earned-revenue
  payment use case; technology services score 3; e-commerce scores 2
  for potentially more varied goods and payment flows. Individual
  businesses may differ materially.
- Evidence strength: every candidate scores 2 because segment-specific
  customer validation and complete cross-border quotes are missing.

## Sensitivity Check

Reducing agency buyer-access and pilot-simplicity scores by one point each lowers its score from 3.80 to 3.45. This ties the technology-service candidate at 3.45.

The initial preference therefore depends on unvalidated access and
simplicity assumptions. It is a working pilot choice, not a robust
market ranking.

## Decision Gate

Carry agencies forward as the provisional candidate for ICP definition.
Confirm that choice in the next step and identify exclusions.

Before a real pilot, validate buyer access, payment needs, eligibility,
operational fit and willingness to evaluate. Re-score all candidates
if evidence contradicts the assumptions.
