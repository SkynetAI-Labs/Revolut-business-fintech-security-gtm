# Adoption Barriers

## Historical Evidence

S005 identifies perceived switching difficulty and complex business-account
charges as potential barriers in its 2022 review. Its cover now explicitly
labels the review historical.

Use this to frame questions, not quantify current customer behaviour.

## Hypotheses for Current Validation

| Potential barrier | What must be checked | Implication for the proposed pilot |
|---|---|---|
| Switching effort | Payment dependencies, customer notifications and migration tasks | Compare a secondary-account trial with a full switch |
| Unclear total cost | Plan fees, allowances, FX and transfer charges for actual usage | Use transparent scenario-based comparisons |
| Eligibility and onboarding | Supported legal forms, sectors, locations and required documents | Qualify prospects before recommending an application |
| Operational fit | Required integrations, controls, currencies and support | Use a requirements checklist rather than feature slogans |
| Trust and continuity | Prospect concerns about access, service and protection | Verify current provider terms and avoid blanket assurances |
| Existing banking relationships | Reliance on facilities or services outside our product scope | Do not recommend closing an existing account without assessing dependencies |

These are analyst hypotheses, not measured prevalence estimates or
verified shortcomings of Revolut Business.

## Validation Approach

- Check current official provider requirements in Stage 3.
- Compare relevant alternatives and usage costs in Stage 4.
- Include barrier questions in draft discovery materials.
- Record unresolved questions explicitly.
- Do not claim customer interviews or successful migrations.
- Do not claim a secondary account removes onboarding or operational risk.

## Current Conclusion

No evidence gathered so far establishes current switching intent or a
ranked list of barriers for our three candidate segments. Those remain
research and customer-validation gaps.
