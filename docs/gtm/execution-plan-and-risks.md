## Proposed 90-Day Schedule

| Period | Work | Deliverable | Decision gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Days 1-15 | Refresh sources, resolve material pricing gaps and check required workflows | Updated evidence and comparison | No material unknown prevents a fair evaluation |
| Days 16-30 | Conduct voluntary discovery if separately authorised | Anonymised findings and revised ICP | At least 5 attended discoveries; 3 independently report a relevant recurring problem |
| Days 31-45 | Test authorised channel activity and messaging | Channel costs, time and qualification records | Relevant interest merits continued testing |
| Days 46-60 | Complete suitable comparisons and track evaluation decisions | Documented costs, requirements and buyer decisions | At least 5 qualified businesses and 2 explicit evaluation decisions |
| Days 61-75 | Follow voluntary evaluations and known provider outcomes | Separate application, approval and activation records | Outcomes remain measurable within resources |
| Days 76-90 | Review costs, funnel outcomes and contradictory evidence | Continue, revise or close recommendation | Assess against the model without assuming profitability |

Numerical gates are planning assumptions, not industry benchmarks.
Day-90 base planning values are 40 enquiries, 20 attended discoveries,
10 qualified businesses, 4 evaluations and 2 activations.

Pending outcomes remain pending. Do not accelerate applications or
classify unknown outcomes as successes to meet a target.

## Risk Register

| Risk | Consequence | Proposed response | Review trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak or absent customer pain | Low relevant demand | Use neutral discovery and retain negative findings | Fewer than 3 of the first 5 attended discoveries report a relevant recurring problem |
| Buyer access assumptions fail | Enquiry targets become unrealistic | Review channel access, message and segment selection | Day-30 or day-60 gate missed |
| Incomplete cross-border pricing | Misleading cost comparison | Obtain comparable quotes or withhold a cost ranking | Material route or fee remains unresolved |
| Eligibility or activity mismatch | Unsuitable evaluation | Check actual activities, ownership and locations | Qualification reveals a scope conflict |
| Integration or permissions mismatch | Operational disruption | Verify required functions before recommending evaluation | Essential workflow cannot be confirmed |
| Additional-account complexity | Benefits offset by administration | Compare full-switch and secondary-account options separately | Added tasks outweigh the buyer's assessed benefit |
| Changing product terms or prices | Outdated conclusions | Recheck material sources and effective dates | Before publication, launch and provider comparison |
| Unknown contractual entity or protection | Inaccurate protection claim | Verify the specific product and current applicable arrangements | Protection comparison requested |
| Small sample and uncertain attribution | Overstated commercial conclusions | Report counts, denominators and limitations | Any continuation or scaling decision |
| Time or cash overrun | Uncontrolled pilot cost | Review scope before exceeding resource limits | Forecast exceeds 75 hours or GBP150 cash |
| Implied provider affiliation | Misleading communication | State independent educational status clearly | Any externally proposed material |
| Unsupported revenue assumptions | False viability claim | Separate gross billings, contribution and portfolio income | Any profitability or break-even conclusion |

## Ownership and Review

A hypothetical pilot lead maintains the evidence, risk and resource
records. The applicant and provider control account application and
approval decisions.

Record each material issue with an owner, next action, review date and
status. Proposed roles do not establish an appointed team or sponsor.

## Final Decision Options

Continue:
Further testing is justified by relevant evidence, suitable workflows
and an explicit resource decision. This does not imply permission to scale.

Revise:
Change the ICP, channel, message or model when evidence challenges an
assumption. Preserve the original assumption and the reason for change.

Close:
Stop when demand, suitability or economics do not justify further work.
An evidence-based negative conclusion is a valid research outcome.
