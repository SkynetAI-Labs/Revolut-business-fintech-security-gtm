# Competitive Positioning — Opportunities and Trade-offs

**Research date:** 23 October 2026
**Status:** Analyst interpretation; not customer-validated
**Evidence:** Competitor features, pricing results, onboarding and
integration comparison; sources S006-S025 where relevant

## Working Opportunity

Test whether UK limited companies with recurring cross-border activity
value a combination of currency management, payments and administrative
controls enough to justify the applicable subscription and usage costs.

This is a hypothesis to evaluate, not evidence of demand or superiority.

## Competitive Implications

| Buyer situation | Implication for the proposed Revolut positioning | Trade-off or evidence gap |
|---|---|---|
| Mainly domestic standard digital payments | Lead with operational requirements only where relevant | Our domestic scenario does not support a cheapest-provider claim |
| Recurring foreign-currency receipts and supplier payments | Offer a transparent comparison of actual routes and conversion needs | Cross-border totals remain unresolved; no savings claim is established |
| Team access and approval requirements | Assess the required controls against the applicable plan | Higher-plan fees and exact permission functions must be checked |
| Accounting administration creates manual work | Explore whether the advertised integration supports the actual workflow | No time-saving measurement or integration trial has been conducted |
| Cash, cheques or in-person support are essential | Include these dependencies in provider selection | Our pricing scenarios exclude them and cannot establish suitability |
| Existing bank services remain necessary | Evaluate a secondary account as a separate option | Added reconciliation, onboarding and account-management effort may offset benefits |

## Proposed Commercial Approach

1. Qualify business activity, ownership, locations and eligibility.
2. Establish payment routes, currencies, volumes and existing costs.
3. Identify required controls, integrations and service channels.
4. Compare equivalent usage and record unresolved charges.
5. Present relevant benefits with their plan costs and limitations.
6. Retain alternatives when they better meet the stated requirements.

## Claims to Avoid

- Cheapest business account or guaranteed savings.
- Guaranteed approval, onboarding speed or payment arrival.
- Identical protection arrangements across providers or products.
- Proven reduction in administrative hours.
- Evidence of customer demand, switching intent or conversion.
- A claim that the portfolio API represents Revolut's actual systems.

## Research Gaps Carried Forward

- Comparable timestamped cross-border quotes and complete route costs.
- Exact integration and permission requirements for the selected segment.
- Current contractual entity and applicable protection for each product
  before making a protection comparison.
- Customer validation of pain, willingness to pay and adoption barriers.
- HSBC pricing applicable after its announced December 2026 change.

These gaps limit the conclusions. They must remain visible in the final
case study; no unsupported ranking should replace them.

## Decision for the Next Stage

Keep all three candidate segments under consideration. Use an explicit
scoring framework to select one provisional target, showing assumptions
and uncertainty rather than presenting the selection as validated demand.
