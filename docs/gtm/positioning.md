# Proposed Positioning

**Research date:** 23 October 2026
**Status:** Hypothetical strategy for an independent portfolio case study
**Target:** Provisional agency ICP; no customer validation conducted

## Positioning Statement

For UK limited-company agencies receiving recurring overseas revenue,
evaluate Revolut Business as an option for managing currency payments
and related financial administration in one business platform.

The proposed reason to consider it is the combination of advertised
currency, payment and administrative capabilities, where the applicable
plan supports the agency's actual requirements.

Selection should depend on verified eligibility, complete usage costs
and operational fit against relevant alternatives.

## Positioning Components

| Component | Proposed approach |
|---|---|
| Audience | UK limited-company agencies with recurring cross-border activity |
| Problem to validate | Difficulty understanding payment costs or administering international flows |
| Category | Business account and financial-management platform |
| Proposed value | Relevant payment and administration capabilities within one platform |
| Evidence | Official product and plan information recorded in the source register |
| Decision condition | Benefits must justify subscription, usage costs and operational change |

## Competitive Boundaries

- The domestic scenario does not support a lowest-cost claim.
- Complete cross-border quotes remain unresolved.
- A combination of features is a reason to evaluate, not proof of
  uniqueness or superiority.
- Other providers or a secondary-account arrangement may fit better.
- Required controls, integrations and service channels must be checked.

## Message to Test in Discovery

Would clearer visibility of international payment costs and a better
fit between payments and accounting workflows justify evaluating
another business account?

This is a research question, not a measured customer preference or
a promise that the product delivers those outcomes.

## Validation Criteria

Retain this positioning only if discovery establishes a meaningful
problem, the required capabilities are available, complete costs are
acceptable and the buyer is willing to evaluate.

Revise it if buyers prioritise other needs or alternatives fit better.
