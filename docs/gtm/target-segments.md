# Candidate Target Segments

**Research date:** 23 October 2026
**Status:** Analyst-defined segments; demand and eligibility unvalidated

## Common Research Boundary

UK limited companies employing 1-49 people, with recurring cross-border
business activity. Employee count is an analytical boundary, not a
provider eligibility rule or proof of commercial attractiveness.

Consider earned business revenue and payments for the company's own
operations. Exclude client-money management from the proposed pilot.

## Candidate Definitions

| Candidate | Activity boundary | Cross-border trigger to investigate | Likely buyer hypothesis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Professional-services agencies | Marketing, design or similar service agencies billing overseas clients for their own work | Recurring foreign-currency invoices or overseas contractor payments | Founder, managing director or finance lead |
| International e-commerce firms | Businesses selling their own permitted goods and paying suppliers | Recurring overseas purchasing or foreign-currency sales receipts | Founder, operations lead or finance lead |
| Software and technology-service firms | Businesses earning revenue from software or technology services | Overseas customer receipts or international operating expenditure | Founder, finance lead or operations lead |

These definitions do not establish provider eligibility. Actual goods,
services, countries, ownership and payment activities require checking.

## What We Must Learn

For each candidate, establish:

1. Frequency, value, currencies and routes of actual payments.
2. Existing total costs and operational friction.
3. Required accounting connections, permissions and approval workflows.
4. Buying trigger, decision owner and practical route to reach buyers.
5. Dependencies that could prevent adoption.
6. Willingness to evaluate and pay for an alternative.

## Scoring Dimensions for the Next Step

- Relevance of cross-border payment needs.
- Fit with verified advertised capabilities.
- Practical access to the buyer.
- Simplicity of qualification and pilot execution.
- Strength of available evidence.

Use the same weights and score definitions for all candidates.
Label judgement-based scores as assumptions and test whether changes
to those scores alter the preferred segment.

## Evidence Boundary

Available broad SME statistics do not quantify these specific eligible,
reachable segments. Do not multiply national business counts by assumed
adoption rates and present the result as a verified market size.

No interviews, prospect lists, outreach or purchase commitments have
been collected.
